Country outlines: Natural Earth
===============================

assets/geo/countries.json is derived from Natural Earth's
"Admin 0 – Countries" set at 1:10m scale, version 5.1.2
(ne_10m_admin_0_countries.geojson from the v5.1.2 tag of
github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector, SHA-256
239eec57ac17f100a11e2536cffc56752c318b50ae765b0918ff7aab4ce8f255).

    https://www.naturalearthdata.com/

Natural Earth places its data in the public domain:

    "All versions of Natural Earth raster and vector map data found on this
    website are in the public domain. You may use the maps in any manner,
    including modifying the content and design, electronic dissemination, and
    offset printing. The primary authors, Tom Patterson and Nathaniel Vaughn
    Kelso, and all other contributors renounce all financial claim to the maps
    and invite you to use them for personal, educational, and commercial
    purposes.

    No permission is needed to use Natural Earth. Crediting the authors is
    unnecessary."

The credit is given anyway: a map is only as truthful as the outlines under
it, and somebody should be able to see where these came from.

What was changed on the way in
------------------------------

The source GeoJSON is 13 MB and carries some eighty attributes per country.
This asset keeps six: the three-letter administrative code, the sovereign state
the area counts toward (its ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, or its three-letter code where
the source has no alpha-2 — Somaliland, Northern Cyprus), the English and German
names, the region, whether the area is self-governing, and the geometry.

All 258 areas are kept and drawn, dependencies included, because a world map
with holes in it is a worse map. What is counted is 195 states: the 193 members
of the United Nations plus the two observer states, Vatican City and Palestine.
A dependency or a leased area counts for the state it belongs to (Greenland for
Denmark, Baikonur for Kazakhstan). Ground of disputed or undetermined
sovereignty counts for none: Kosovo, Taiwan, Northern Cyprus, Somaliland,
Western Sahara, Antarctica, Siachen Glacier, Brazilian Island, Bir Tawil, the
Spratly Islands and the other reefs and banks the source leaves undetermined.

The source names the Spratly Islands "Wake Island" (German: "Wake"); this asset
corrects that to "Spratly Islands" / "Spratly-Inseln".

`tool/build_country_outlines.dart` in this repository is the conversion.

Rings are stored as encoded polylines at three decimal places — about 110 m —
and simplified with a Douglas-Peucker tolerance scaled to each ring's own
extent (at most 0.005°, about 350 m), which brings the file to 1.0 MB. The
scaling is the point: one fixed tolerance loose enough to be worth applying to
Russia's coastline collapses San Marino to a triangle and Monaco to nothing at
all.

Regions are the source's own REGION_UN, except that the Americas are split by
CONTINENT into North and South America, and South Georgia is filed under South
America rather than Antarctica.

Two consequences worth knowing
------------------------------

A generalized outline lies a little off the ground it stands for, and below a
certain size that is the whole country. Measured against real coordinates at
this scale, San Marino, Liechtenstein, Andorra, Singapore, Malta, the Maldives
and Monaco are found; the Vatican is not — its outline stops short of St
Peter's Square, so a point there falls in Italy.

The same effect puts a coastal position outside its own country's outline, and
in a port it is common: even unsimplified, the source leaves Copenhagen's
central station 0.14 km out to sea. Such a point counts for the nearest country
when that is within 3 km and at most half as far as any other; otherwise it
counts for none, because a missing country is a gap and a wrong one is a claim.

That is why a country can also be ticked by hand.
